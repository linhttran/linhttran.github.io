The U.S. is facing a large and persistent treatment gap in mental health services. Administrative frictions exacerbate this dynamic. For providers, administrative frictions, such as onerous credentialing process to join insurance networks and complex administrative tasks, contribute to burnout and reluctance to accept health insurance. For patients, persistent inaccuracies in provider directories prevent them from getting insurance-covered mental health services. This project examines how alleviating administrative frictions impacts the market for mental health services by studying the rise of large online mental health practices (hereafter "LOMPs"), platforms that organize providers under a single group practice and contract with payers on their behalf, and provide a convenient platform for patients to search and match with in-network providers. Using a state's all payer claims database, I examine the effects of LOMPs on providers' practice patterns, such as patient panels and reimbursements, and on patients' access to mental health services and quality of care.
