As mental health crises become increasingly common and contribute to overcrowding of emergency departments (ED), states are expanding mental health crisis care as a community-based and cost-effective alternative to the ED. Understanding utilization patterns and outcomes for patients receiving care at crisis care centers versus the ED provides crucial evidence as policymakers consider funding these services. Using Medicaid claims from the 2021 Transformed Medicaid Statistical Information System Analytic Files, we explore how characteristics of people using crisis care differ from those using emergency department for mental health-relation reasons in Arizona. We identify a set of crisis centers and compare the distribution of patient diagnoses and demographics among mental health-related or substance use-related episodes involving a crisis center, an emergency department, or both. Among mental health-related episodes, those involving crisis care are more likely to have diagnoses of schizophrenia and bipolar disorder than ED-only episodes. Among substance use-related episodes, opioid and stimulant disorders were more common among crisis episodes.
