Low insurance acceptance rates among psychiatrists may require patients to pay fully out-of-pocket (“cash pay”) for care, potentially imposing financial burden and deterring treatment. Yet, research quantifying the prevalence of cash pay among psychiatry patients and describing the characteristics of those patients is limited. Using Medicare claims for a random 20% sample of traditional Medicare beneficiaries prescribed mental health medications by a psychiatrist or primary care physician (PCP) between 2016 and 2020, we develop a novel algorithm to identify patients likely paying cash for psychiatrist services and describe their demographic and clinical characteristics. 15.2% of Medicare beneficiaries who saw a psychiatrist likely paid cash for their visits, compared to 2.5% of PCP patients receiving similar medications. Patients who likely paid cash for psychiatrist services were more likely to reside in high-income areas and had prescriptions and diagnoses consistent with less serious mental illnesses. The prevalence of paying cash for psychiatrist services varied more than 7-fold across metropolitan areas. The findings underscore potential inequities in care allocation and highlight the need for ongoing policy efforts to improve insurance acceptance among psychiatrists.
